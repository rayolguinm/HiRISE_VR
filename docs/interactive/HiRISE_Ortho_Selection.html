HiRISE Ortho Selection

Author Raymundo "R@y" OlguinHiRISE Operations Center (HiROC), Lunar and Planetary Laboratory, University of Arizona

A HiRISE DTM project usually holds four orthoimages: both images of the stereo pair, each at its full resolution and as a reduced 1 m copy. Only one becomes the texture of the 3D mesh. The pipeline chooses the one that puts the most real detail in the right place on the terrain, and never one with holes when a complete one exists.

The principles come from the HiRISE instrument and DTM literature (McEwen, Kirk, Sutton, Chojnacki and colleagues). The thresholds and the single score below are this pipeline's synthesis of those principles, written so every choice can be checked. They should be confirmed with the HiROC photogrammetry lead before the rule itself is cited.

E = √( g² + (Δz · tan e)² )
g
ortho grid spacing: A 0.25 m, B 0.5 m, C 1.0 m
Δz
DTM vertical precision expected for its post spacing (m): 0.3 m at 1 m posts, 1.0 m at 2 m posts (Sutton et al. 2022). The README "RMS z" is the residual of the tie to MOLA, not the precision, so it is not used (v6.3.7).
e
emission angle of the source image, from its label
E
effective ground error in metres; the smallest wins

What is in a project folder

HiROC orthorectifies each image of the stereo pair onto the DTM made from that same pair, so every ortho in the folder shares the DTM's geometry. The letter in the file name is the grid spacing. The binning of the source image sets its real pixel size, and so which letter carries its full detail.

The method, step by step

Each step only narrows what the previous one kept. The order encodes the priorities: a hole is worse than blur, and blur and misplacement are weighed in the same unit.

  1. 0

    Inventory

    For every ortho JP2: observation ID, colour band and grid letter from the file name; emission and incidence angles, MRO:BINNING and MAP_SCALE from the labels; the DTM's vertical RMS error from the README.

    Every later decision is made from these numbers, and all of them are written to the selection record.

    BasisHiRISE DTM README; Sutton et al. 2022
  2. 1

    Eligibilityfilter

    RED orthos only. Unreadable or blank files are rejected.

    RED is the full-swath, full-resolution channel; the colour channels cover a narrower strip and are usually binned.

    BasisMcEwen et al. 2007
  3. 2

    Native samplingfilter

    Per image, keep only the ortho at the image's real pixel size: bin 1 → A, bin 2 → B, bin 4 → C. A finer grid is dropped; a coarser grid becomes a backup.

    A finer grid only interpolates. A coarser grid throws away detail the mesh cannot carry: a HiRISE DTM resolves only about 10 to 20 image pixels horizontally, so everything finer exists only in the texture. Evidence order: binning in a label, then a source-image MAP_SCALE, then the finest grid HiROC produced for that image (an _ORTHO.LBL scale describes the ortho grid, not the image).

    Since v6.3.9 the reports also give each source image's true pixel size from the PDS RDR cumulative index: slant distance × 1 µrad IFOV × binning (McEwen et al. 2007). At MRO's 250 to 320 km slant range a bin-1 pixel is 0.25 to 0.32 m (0.32 m for ESP_044810_2620), so the 0.25 m A grid is the native grid or slightly finer than it, never coarser. The selection rule is unchanged; the same pixel size now sets the stereo precision EP.

    BasisMcEwen et al. 2007 (binning modes, SNR); Kirk et al. 2020 (effective DTM resolution)
  4. 3

    Coverage of the DTMfilter

    Coverage = valid DTM posts where the ortho has real pixels ÷ all valid DTM posts, sampled at the DTM's own posts. Candidates more than 1 percentage point below the most complete one become backups.

    One image of a pair can have missing data that the other does not. A hole is a total loss of texture, worse than any sub-metre difference ranked later. Sampling at the DTM posts ignores the black frame around every HiRISE strip, which the old test counted as empty.

    Basispipeline synthesis; orthos share the DTM grid (Sutton et al. 2022)
  5. 4

    Geometric fidelityoverride + measure

    Images flagged for spacecraft jitter (--jitter-obs or a JITTER.txt in the project) are used only if nothing else is left. For the rest, the sideways misplacement on the DTM is Δz · tan e.

    Jitter distorts HiRISE pushbroom images and can disturb stereo matching and the orthoimages. A DTM height error shifts the draped image by Δz · tan e; orthoimage registration offsets are part of the uncertainty in HiRISE change measurements.

    BasisSutton et al. 2017 (jitter); Kirk et al. 2008 (stereo geometry); Chojnacki et al. 2019 (registration offsets)
  6. 5

    Scorerank

    E = √(g² + (Δz · tan e)²) for each remaining ortho; the smallest wins. Δz is the expected precision for the post spacing: 0.3 m for 1 m DTMs and 1.0 m for 2 m DTMs. The README "RMS z" is a control residual to MOLA (Sutton et al. 2022, Sec. 2.1.3) and is not used.

    Blur from the pixel size and misplacement from the viewing angle are independent errors in the same unit, metres on the ground, so they add in quadrature.

    BasisDTM precision: tens of cm at 1 m posts, about 1 m at 2 m posts (Sutton et al. 2022)
  7. 6

    Radiometric tie-breakrank

    Within 5 % of the best E: lower emission, then fewer saturated pixels, then higher coverage.

    The near-nadir image is the one the stereo plan designs as the mapping image; signal-to-noise depends on binning and TDI.

    BasisMcEwen et al. 2007
  8. 7

    Fallback and record

    If the winner fails to convert, the next in rank is used, backups included. Every candidate, its numbers and the decision go to JP2_CONVERTED/ORTHO_SELECTION.csv, the run log and the technical reports.

    A recorded choice can be reproduced and reviewed, which matters when orthos feed measurements such as change detection.

    Basisprovenance practice; Sutton et al. 2022; Chojnacki et al. 2019

The same rows go to JP2_CONVERTED/ORTHO_SELECTION.csv with these columns: rank, selected, file, observation, band, grid letter and spacing, binning, native letter and how it was decided, role, emission and incidence angles, coverage, saturated pixels, jitter flag, Δz and its source, misplacement, score E and the decision in words. The HTML technical report shows the table in its ortho section, and the PDF and LaTeX reports describe the method.

Using it

Nothing changes in how you run the pipeline. Two optional inputs exist for jitter, which cannot be measured reliably from an ortho alone:

python -m obj_pipeline --input "D:\HiRISE\DTEEC_010039_2220_009538_2220_A01" --formats all --jitter-obs ESP_083055_1630

# or, per project, a file named JITTER.txt next to the DTM:
ESP_083055_1630    # example ID: one observation per line, # starts a comment

Limits

The 1 percentage point coverage tolerance, the 5 % tie band and the assumed Δz values are choices made for this pipeline, not published HiROC thresholds. Jitter is a manual flag. Shadow and haze are not scored beyond saturation, and both images of a pair usually share similar illumination. The score ranks how faithfully the texture sits on the DTM; it does not judge which image is prettier.
